What the argument proves: she was on the gallery while he hung; she chose the pages; she did not ring; and, since the jacket was planted (2026-09-02), that hands were on his chest before he went through the bay. What only her confession supplies: whether she meant it, and the two words he said. Ward's "Did it change?" / "Then that's the one" is the verdict on the account; the rail is on the page.

## E. Three independent chains to Ruth

1. **Custody of paper.** Bell's three layers (Ch12) → nothing on the gallery (Ch12) → the overlays and the drawer (Ch13). Anyone who took the pages was on the gallery after the fall.
2. **The stair.** Whiting (Ch5, Ch12) → two sets of tracks, one turning back (Ch12) → the smear on Ruth's shoe (Ch4, Ch12). Only Ruth came down.
3. **Her own words.** "He said not to wait" (Ch4) → "He needed time" (Ch6) → "Help me... I said no" (Ch9) → "Nobody said anything" (Ch14). The accounts contradict each other before any physical evidence is applied.

The button (Ch5, Ch12) converts presence into choice; the tear (Ch13) converts choice into struggle; the jacket (Ch12) converts struggle into a push; the confession supplies intent.
