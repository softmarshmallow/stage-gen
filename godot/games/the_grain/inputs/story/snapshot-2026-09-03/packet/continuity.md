## C. Decided and kept

- **Frances** (Ch1) stays unexplained. It is the old case's texture and the only thing in Chapter One that is purely theirs.
- **Chapter Sixteen is titled "Tuesday" and opens on Monday.** The title is the last word of the book and the day Henry has to offer; the chapter's four scenes walk Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, and the following Tuesday on purpose. Keep.
- **"Mannequins in fur stand in October" (Ch14)** is literal: the Sunday is 1 October. Keep.
- **Arrival route of Marian, Nell, and Paul** is never stated (Hill Street door, service lift; Bell admitting). The text calls it "the elevator." Ward's closed-building speech covers everything after closing, which is all that matters. Keep implicit.
- **"Two transfers" (Ch16) vs. "working transfers of six tapes" and "the safety transfer" (Ch10).** Nell is listing categories at speed; leave.
- **The clip.** Bell saw the loose set "under a clip"; Ruth says it had come off; Ward finds no clip on the gallery. It went into her bag with the pile and is beneath the notice of an inventory that counts sheets. Keep.
- **Paul "unlocks" the service lift with the brass key (Ch4)** though the key is only needed below cosmetics. A man turning a key in a lift he has been handed a key for; leave.

**Decided and applied (2026-09-02, on "let's continue").** (1) The push is planted: Ward lists two whiting marks on the front of Paul's jacket on Friday (Ch12 Sc37) and says nothing about them; Henry reads them on Monday (Ch15 Sc48) before Ruth confesses, so every step of the arrest is now on the page and the confession supplies only the interior. (2) Scene 50 stays; the jail scene depends on it ("She said no to Havel. Once."). (3) A first voice pass over Chapters Seven, Eleven, and Fourteen: bare "Yes."/"No." from the three leads reduced where a bare word was a habit and not a blow (Ruth's lies stay monosyllables on purpose), Nell given one sentence that runs on under pressure (Ch7 Sc15), Henry's one "That was not the question" (Ch11) replaced so the correction-of-the-question belongs to Nell and Ward. The three notes as they stood:

1. **The push.** Both the pass-2 editor and this reader say the push is confession-only. The reader's one change is to cut it (arrest for what the evidence shows: she held him, saw the bell, chose the pages, let go) or to plant it. The book's design since Act Three was a will-she-say-it, with Ward's "Did it change?" as the verdict. Options: (a) keep, and accept that the last step is hers; (b) cut the push and let "I let go with the other hand" be the crime; (c) plant one thing Henry can say first (a whiting print on the chest of Paul's coat is the cheapest, since her hands had been on the planks; Ward would list it Friday with the shoes). My recommendation is (c): it keeps the shape and removes the only step a reader can call unearned.
2. **Scene 50 (Havel, Tuesday).** The reader would cut it and give "permission" to Henry in the jail. The pass-2 editor kept it ("each scene has one job"). A taste call.
3. **The leads' shared instrument.** Counts: 87 bare "Yes." (Henry 32, Ruth 21), 64 "No." (Ruth 24), 17 "I know.", ten "that is not what I asked" variants across four speakers, eleven "All right," ten "Good," ten "Tomorrow." This is the same note as pass 1, now measured. A voice pass over Chapters Seven, Eleven, and Fourteen, giving Nell longer sentences and Henry fewer corrections of the question, is the next revision if the book is not resting.
