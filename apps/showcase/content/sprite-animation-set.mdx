A side-view game character needs a dozen animations, and every one of them has to be the same
character, at the same size, with its feet on the same line. Drawn one at a time, by hand or by an
image model, they drift: the run comes back smaller than the idle, one pose runs into the next, the
feet sit at a different height in every frame. Stage Gen draws the character once as a concept from
the game's cover and a short brief, then draws each animation from that concept as one row of poses. A local gate cuts every pose out whole and stands it on one line. A vision model then compares
all the strips on one plate and sets each one's size against idle, and a second pass checks the
result. The game gets a strip per animation and the numbers to play it: its frames, its speed,
whether it loops, which way it faces, and its size.

The content checkpoint also draws the game's creatures, townsfolk and items; the review is paid for separately, in its own checkpoint.

## Where it works

<Scope>
  <Works>A side-view character for a platformer or a side-scrolling action game</Works>
  <Works>One reference picture, such as the game's cover, and a short brief</Works>
  <Works>The usual set: idle, walk, run, jump, crouch, two climbs, an attack, a skill, hurt and death</Works>
  <Works>A character who carries one hand weapon through every animation</Works>
  <Limit>Top-down or isometric games: this is side view only, mirrored for left, with no up or down facings</Limit>
  <Limit>Smooth motion: each animation is four key poses, two for the climbs, with no in-betweens</Limit>
  <Limit>Changing equipment: the sword is drawn into every frame, so a new weapon means drawing the set again</Limit>
  <Tip>The reviewer rejected this set, and the game ships it anyway, because the review is advice. Two of its three reasons came from its own instructions, which say every strip has four frames while the climbs are defined with two. The third is fair: the sword is missing from the dialogue portraits.</Tip>
  <Tip>Apart from the gate, only the two size judgements can stop a run.</Tip>
  <Tip>Idle holds one frame. Four were drawn; the other three are spare.</Tip>
  <Tip>Climbing frames follow the distance climbed, not a clock, so the character stops moving when the player stops.</Tip>
</Scope>

## What goes wrong by hand

<Guards>
  <Guard node="player-wayfarer-motion-rebase" how="A vision model compares every strip with idle on one plate, by head width and line weight, and sets a size for each.">Each animation comes back at its own size, so the character grows when she starts to run.</Guard>
  <Guard node="player-wayfarer-motion-rebase-verify" how="The plate is drawn again with those sizes applied and judged once more, and what is left is corrected.">A size set by eye is still a little off, and nobody checks it twice.</Guard>
  <Guard node="player-wayfarer-state-run-validate" how="Each pose is cut out whole and stood on one line in evenly spaced cells.">Poses sit at different heights in their cells, and the feet bob or slide.</Guard>
  <Guard node="player-wayfarer-state-skill_cast-validate" how="The gate refuses a strip with a missing pose or poses it cannot tell apart. In this cast two poses touched at their soft edges, and it separated them at their solid cores.">A pose is missing, or one pose runs into the next.</Guard>
  <Guard node="player-wayfarer-concept-generate" how="Every animation is drawn from the one concept picture, not from the brief alone.">The scarf, the satchel or the sword changes from one animation to the next.</Guard>
</Guards>
