A side-scrolling level is drawn in layers: a sky, a far horizon, a midground, and a band in front of
the player. The camera moves each one at its own speed, and that difference is what reads as depth.
Every layer has to repeat without end, and a strip whose two ends do not meet shows a seam once
every period. Mirroring the strip hides the seam and shows every landmark twice, facing itself.
Stage Gen draws each layer from the map's reference picture and tests it as it came back. A layer
that does not loop has its own wrap moved to the middle of a canvas the image model repaints, and
the repaint is cut back in along the path where it agrees with the drawing. The wrap and both cuts
are measured against the layer's own columns, and the layer is trimmed and placed for the game.

## Where it works

<Scope>
  <Works>A side-scrolling level drawn in a few depth layers, far to near</Works>
  <Works>Cut-out layers with transparency, and opaque backdrops</Works>
  <Works>Any scroll speed, including a band in front that passes faster than the ground</Works>
  <Works>A layer the model already draws as a loop, which is published untouched at no extra cost</Works>
  <Limit>Loops across only; nothing repeats vertically</Limit>
  <Limit>A landmark standing right on the wrap can be painted over by the repaint</Limit>
  <Limit>A plain gradient: the model cannot repaint one to within a level of its own steps, so a gradient sky falls back to its reflection. An empty gradient reflected is the same picture, and these skies never move.</Limit>
  <Tip>Two of the six moving layers, the garden fence and the bell-tree route, came back already looping, and cost nothing more.</Tip>
  <Tip>A join passes when it steps no more than the layer's own columns do. A fixed limit either misses a flat seam or refuses the detail of the art itself.</Tip>
  <Tip>The reviewer passed both maps' looping, ground and layering. It still rejected them: Sunpetal lacks the bridge and stream its reference shows, and on both maps it judged the portal and ladder sheets at their full drawn size rather than as the game places them.</Tip>
  <Tip>Each layer's size and height come from the game's own map files, so the far peaks stand behind the village and nothing shows between the layers even without the ground.</Tip>
</Scope>

## What goes wrong by hand

<Guards>
  <Guard node="map-sunpetal-crossing-layer-sunpetal_village-loop" how="The wrap is moved to the middle of a canvas the model repaints, and the repaint is cut back in along the path where it agrees with the drawing.">The two ends of a strip do not meet, and a seam passes by once every period.</Guard>
  <Guard node="map-crowncrag-road-layer-amberbell_horizon-loop" how="The period stays the width that was drawn. Nothing is appended or reflected.">Mirroring the strip hides the seam, and every landmark shows up twice, facing itself.</Guard>
  <Guard node="map-sunpetal-crossing-layer-sunpetal_village-validate" how="The wrap and both cuts are measured against the layer's own columns, and the loop is checked again after trimming.">A seam is judged by eye, at one zoom, on one screen.</Guard>
  <Guard node="map-crowncrag-road-layer-amberbell_route-loop" how="Every layer is tested first; one that already loops is published untouched.">Every layer is repainted, whether it needed it or not.</Guard>
  <Guard node="map-sunpetal-crossing-review" how="A board of every layer and the ground is judged by a model that drew none of it.">Nobody looks at the layers together.</Guard>
</Guards>
