A side-view game character needs a dozen animations, and every one of them has to be the same character, at the same size, with its feet on the same line. Drawn one at a time, by hand or by an image model, they drift: the run comes back smaller than the idle, one pose runs into the next, the feet sit at a different height in every frame. Stage Gen draws the character once as a concept from the game's cover and a short brief, then draws each animation from that concept as one row of poses. A local gate cuts every pose out whole and stands it on one line. A vision model then compares all the strips on one plate and sets each one's size against idle, and a second pass checks the result. The game gets a strip per animation and the numbers to play it: its frames, its speed, whether it loops, which way it faces, and its size.

Where it works

Works well

  • A side-view character for a platformer or a side-scrolling action game
  • One reference picture, such as the game's cover, and a short brief
  • The usual set: idle, walk, run, jump, crouch, two climbs, an attack, a skill, hurt and death
  • A character who carries one hand weapon through every animation

Not expected to work

  • Top-down or isometric games: this is side view only, mirrored for left, with no up or down facings
  • Smooth motion: each animation is four key poses, two for the climbs, with no in-betweens
  • Changing equipment: the sword is drawn into every frame, so a new weapon means drawing the set again

Good to know

  • The reviewer rejected this set, and the game ships it anyway, because the review is advice. Two of its three reasons came from its own instructions, which say every strip has four frames while the climbs are defined with two. The third is fair: the sword is missing from the dialogue portraits.
  • Apart from the gate, only the two size judgements can stop a run.
  • Idle holds one frame. Four were drawn; the other three are spare.
  • Climbing frames follow the distance climbed, not a clock, so the character stops moving when the player stops.

Concept

The character is drawn once, in side view and three-quarter view, from the game's cover and a short brief. Every later drawing is held to this picture.

Moving

Each animation is its own image call from the concept: four poses in one row. The gate cuts every pose out whole and stands it on one line.

Climbing, acting and reacting

The climbs are two frames each, seen from behind and hanging from the hands; the game steps them by how far she climbs. Attack, cast, hurt and death are four poses each, played once.

Dialogue

Five expressions for conversations, from the same concept.

One size

Every call draws at its own scale. A vision model compares all the strips with idle on one plate and sets a size for each; a second pass judges the plate with those sizes applied and corrects what is left.

Review

A contact sheet of the whole set, judged by a model that drew none of it. The verdict is advice for the author; it does not stop the run.
