A character that breathes and shifts its weight usually means a video model, a green screen, a keyer,
a loop editor, and someone checking frame by frame that the face did not drift and the edges are not
green. Stage Gen holds that as one graph. The picture is fitted on a green plate and sent once to a
video model, with the same picture at both ends. The take you choose is finished locally: keyed to
transparency, the face held still so blinks and mouth shapes can be added separately, the loop closed,
and every frame checked. The first frame of the finished loop becomes the picture the face workflow
starts from.

## Try it

<Try>
<Agent>

Use Stage Gen's movie sprite workflow in `docs/movie-sprite.md` to turn <Ref input="character">this character</Ref> into a transparent idle loop. Keep the face still, because I will add blinks separately.

Plan it offline, generate one eight-second 720p take with a $2 limit, and show me the take. Finish it only after I say it is good.

</Agent>
<Shell>

```sh
uv run stage-gen run movie-sprite \
  --input-root inputs --authoring authoring.json --finish finish.json \
  --output-root runs/take-01 --cache-root cache \
  --resolution 720p --aspect-ratio 9:16 --duration 8 --target generate \
  --candidate take-01 --live --budget-root budget --budget-usd 2
```

```sh
uv run stage-gen run movie-sprite --replay \
  --input-root inputs --authoring authoring.json --finish finish.json \
  --output-root runs/finished-01 --cache-root cache \
  --resolution 720p --aspect-ratio 9:16 --duration 8 --candidate take-01
```

Needs FFmpeg and a fal key. `authoring.json` names the picture and the motion, and `finish.json` holds the finishing settings; the [guide](https://github.com/softmarshmallow/stage-gen/blob/main/docs/movie-sprite.md) shows both.

</Shell>
</Try>

## Where it works

<Scope>
  <Works>A finished character picture with a transparent background</Works>
  <Works>Gentle idle motion: breathing, a little weight shift, hair and sleeves settling</Works>
  <Works>Characters with no green in their design</Works>
  <Works>Portrait or landscape takes of three to ten seconds, played back slower if you like</Works>
  <Limit>Walking, turning, big gestures, or anything that leaves the pose</Limit>
  <Limit>Green clothing, hair or props: green is what gets removed</Limit>
  <Limit>Exact control of the motion: the prompt guides it, and some takes will not be usable</Limit>
  <Limit>Sound</Limit>
  <Tip>The video model may redraw the character slightly. The loop's first frame becomes the new picture for the face workflow.</Tip>
  <Tip>Pick a take before finishing. Finishing is local, so trying other finishing settings costs nothing.</Tip>
  <Tip>MP4 previews have no transparency. Use the lossless loop or the PNG frames in a game.</Tip>
</Scope>

## What goes wrong by hand

<Guards>
  <Guard node="prepare" how="Length, resolution, aspect ratio and prompt size are checked against the video route before any call.">Settings the video service cannot take are found after paying.</Guard>
  <Guard node="generate" how="One owner handles retries, with budget reserved for every call. A refusal stops at once, and a new take has to be asked for by name.">A failed request is retried blindly and paid for again.</Guard>
  <Guard node="finish" how="Green is removed with edge despill, and the result is checked on light, dark and checkerboard grounds.">A green fringe stays around the hair and edges.</Guard>
  <Guard node="finish" how="The ends are blended with optical flow, and the first and last frames are verified identical.">The loop jumps where it restarts.</Guard>
  <Guard node="finish" how="The face is held to the first frame, and that frame is handed to the face workflow.">The face drifts, so blinks drawn later no longer line up.</Guard>
</Guards>
