# The Grain — ratified story foundation

Status: **selected foundation, incomplete story**.

## Creative promise

- A substantial point-and-click murder mystery built to be desirable as a game,
  not merely useful as a technical demonstration.
- Mostly clever, strongly human, and lightly tense.
- Emotionally painful but restrained, ending on a bittersweet truth.
- Fair-play deduction: the player receives the facts needed to solve the case.
- The murderer may be emotionally understandable but remains accountable.
- No graphic horror, broad farce, arbitrary puzzles, withheld-fact solution,
  inheritance dispute, or eccentric-rich-family imitation.

## World and dramatic direction

- Los Angeles, 1972, during the New Hollywood transition.
- The wider story concerns one significant director and one troubled film.
- Film is the story's hidden gravity, not its opening surface. The game does not
  begin at a studio, theatre, set, screening, or production office.
- The film world should supply glamour, pressure, and thematic resonance without
  reducing every character to an industry function.
- Character relationships and dialogue carry the drama. Intersecting agendas,
  subtext, favors, and rare consequential violence matter more than imitation of
  any reference's plot or voice.

## Protagonist

- A male private investigator in his late forties.
- Quiet, deeply attentive, discreet, and capable of economical wit.
- He knows the director only by reputation and is not hired as the production's
  consultant, insurer, security, or undercover investigator.
- People find him attractive because they feel heard in his presence.
- His flaw is distance: he understands other people's choices so well that he
  avoids committing himself. The story must make him responsible for what he
  comes to know.

## Opening structure

- The detective is asked by a lightly known acquaintance to accompany them to a
  private farewell supper: “Come with me. I need one person there who will not
  perform for them.”
- The supper takes place after closing in the top-floor winter dining room of a
  fictional metropolitan department store.
- The detective attends as a person, not as a professional investigator.
- The player has time to meet the guests and explore before a murder occurs near
  the end of the opening act.
- Immediate evidence makes the crime appear rooted in the farewell gathering.
  Only afterward does the investigation widen honestly toward the troubled film.
- The film connection should feel inevitable in retrospect, never preserved by
  characters unnaturally refusing to name ordinary facts.

## Ensemble and characterization

- The likely central ensemble contains about eight people including the
  detective and spans mixed adult generations.
- The opening quartet consists structurally of the detective, the acquaintance
  who brings him, the departing guest, and the department-store host.
- The detective and companion have an unfinished friendship rather than a
  required romance.
- The departing person is loved differently by several people; affection may be
  possessive without making that person saintly.
- Two opening characters may have separate connections to the troubled film,
  while the host remains genuinely outside the industry.
- Characters are particular people with desires, faults, leverage, tenderness,
  and accountability. They are not demographic tokens or vehicles for
  present-day political lectures.

## Writing form

The canonical narrative manuscript is a dialogue-first playable screenplay in
plain Markdown. Scenes are written first as strong continuous dramatic
conversations with brief present-tense action, silence, interruption, and
subtext. A later pass adds optional questions, environmental discoveries,
knowledge-dependent responses, and alternate order. Ren'Py, JSON, or another
runtime form is a downstream transcription after narrative lock.

## Ratified executable narrative

- `scenarios/chapter_one.scenario` contains the linear scenario-v1 adaptation of
  Chapter One, *One Person There*.
- Its paired declaration is `scenarios/chapter_one.toml`, cataloged by
  `scenarios/index.toml`.
- This ratifies the chapter's current names and spoken text for continued
  development. It does not select a victim, culprit, branch structure, visual
  assets, or complete game package.
