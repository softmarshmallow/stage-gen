# Episode One — The Winter Room

Status: **outline, provisional, 2026-09-03.** Adapted from the novel's Chapters One to
Six (Scenes 1–14) under `../topology.md`. Nothing here is engine data.

## In one paragraph

Wednesday 27 September 1972, from 5:37 in the afternoon to after one in the morning.
Ruth Ellery asks Henry Calder to sit at a farewell supper and not perform. He attends,
at a round table for eight on the top floor of a closed department store, and does the
only thing he is there to do: he pays attention. Where he puts it is the game. At nine a
man goes down in the service lift with his sister and does not come back up; at half
past, Henry is kneeling beside him inside an unfinished display window. By one in the
morning Henry has told a detective what he saw — only what he saw — and has learned
what it costs to have been looking elsewhere. He leaves alone. Nothing branches. What
differs, at the end, is what Henry carries out of the building.

## What the player does

Three things, and nothing else:

- **Attention.** At the table the room offers more than one thing at once. Henry
  follows one. What he watches he sees, and what he sees he can later say.
- **Looking.** In two rooms — the motor court on the way in, the window after — Henry
  looks at things. Each look is a fact.
- **The statement.** Ward asks Henry what happened. Henry can answer only from the
  board; at each beat he can give what he saw, give what he thinks, or keep it. Ward
  hears the difference.

No accusation, no puzzle, no inventory beyond a pocketknife, no fork. The episode's
verdict is a statement, not a solution.

## The walls (from the bible)

- The hand in the door is not a choice. Henry stops the lift door for a man he has never
  met, and when Ward asks why, the only answer is "I don't know." The game does not
  make the player explain it either.
- Henry does not go down with Paul and Ruth. Paul says "Come with me" to Ruth, not to
  him; Ruth says "Watch my coffee." He watches the indicator reach three.
- Between 9:02 and 9:18 Henry is in the Winter Room. The vestibule, the stair, and the
  gallery are the novel's, unseen.
- Henry may say **less** than the novel's Henry, to anyone. He may not say **more** in
  Ruth's hearing about the gallery, the marks, the button, or the stair. Her Wednesday
  account is bible; it is her answer to what she believes is known.
- The fixed sentences are said as written: "I remembered you were a liar." "Watch my
  coffee." "He said not to wait." "Would you have come?" (three times, counted).

## Movements

### I. The office (Scene 1) — conversation, straight

Played as written, no choices. It exists to prove the read on screen: two people, two
cups, a card in a frosted-glass door. The player learns the voice before the game asks
anything of them. One thing is planted for the board without a look: Henry's "You said
you would rather I heard it from you than found it," and her "I remember what I said."

### II. The way in (Scene 2) — room, then conversation

The motor court after closing, the first room. Henry can look before Ruth rings the
bell:

- the unfinished window: six figures facing an empty seventh place under a white paper
  moon;
- the short black rectangle left open above the moon, where the scenery meets the
  ceiling;
- a seamstress's chalk and a pair of scissors on the floor inside the glass;
- the other windows: dinners, painted staircases, luggage.

Then Edwin, the service door, the dark cosmetics floor (Ruth does not look at the
counters; Henry may), the service lift and its floors, the Winter Room, Lydia writing
cards in green ink, the unopened suitcase by the bar. Edwin's "Miss Shaw has moved your
place card twice" is given; it is the thread the coffee movement picks up.

What the looks buy: the window *before*. A Henry who looked can tell the officer, and
Ward, that the moon was whole and the rail was already open at a quarter to eight. A
Henry who did not can say only that he walked past it.

### III. The table (Scenes 3–5) — conversation with attention

**Arrivals** run as written. The one look is the eighth place card, turned toward
Lydia while seven face outward; Lydia's two fingers on it before Ruth can turn it. Nell
asks the question in the novel; here Henry may have seen it first, and the difference
is whether "Her reaction said no" is later a guess or a report.

**The first course.** Three strands, one followed:

| Strand | What is there | What it yields |
|---|---|---|
| The Holts | Robert's soup questions; Marian's salt, taken and not used and set by Robert's hand; the Burbank room with the painted window; "Pasadena end of Burbank" | The Holts' key, and thirty dollars for curtains |
| Ruth and Paul | Ruth tears a roll and puts half on Paul's plate, and knows it only when her hand is empty; Paul sees and says nothing | What the two of them are to each other, before anyone says it |
| Nell and Lydia | Nell eating quickly, Lydia watching and then deliberately not; "What color is the front door?" and "Whose house?"; the last roll, passed and halved | Dark blue, a house, "Mine, beginning Thursday" — which pays a card from Portland in Chapter Sixteen |

Edwin's toast and the laughter are the table's; everyone hears them. Ruth's "Then try to
look less employed" closes the course whichever strand Henry followed.

**The main course.** The pocketknife is Henry's one object and his one act: he may offer
it for Paul's string, or not. If he does, Paul cuts, closes it, and returns it, and Henry
has seen how the man handles a small thing. (It pays on Thursday, at a dismantled fan.)

Two looks sit inside the course's best conversation, and that is the cost:

- When Lydia says "Don't improve it just because it's over," **Ruth's knife touches her
  plate.** Paul hears it; Lydia hears it. Henry hears it only if he is watching Ruth and
  not Lydia telling the story. Without this look, Henry has no reason to say "You've
  heard it," and the coffee conversation about June's sentence never opens.
- While the cinnamon tin travels, **Edwin puts a brass key beside Paul's fork** and says
  "after dessert." Henry sees it if he is watching Edwin's round and not the tin. Without
  it, Henry knows at coffee only that Paul *has* a key, not when he was given it or on
  what condition.

Heard by all, no look required: the capers; "Mostly husbands"; the blanket that follows
you home; the burned chicken and "Eat the middle"; the cream envelope Nell reads upside
down, "Hear it," "After dinner. Alone. You begin."

### IV. Coffee (Scenes 6–7) — occlusion, then the return

Edwin opens a roof panel and the round table stops being the shape of the room. Four
places, one Henry:

| Place | Who | What happens there |
|---|---|---|
| The service bar | Ruth, Paul, then Henry | Ruth's coffee with milk and Paul's extra drop; "—if I say it badly." "Then you said it." "Not this one."; Paul's cup left by Edwin's hand |
| Edwin's end of the bar | Edwin, Robert, Paul | Strong coffee; "Thank you for making both remarks"; orange ice stopped in 1968 |
| The roof panel | Nell, Marian | The open air; Paul repeating the orange ice for Nell; "I heard." "Good." |
| The saucers | Marian, Lydia, then Robert | The place cards fanned; a number in green ink crossed out twice; "I wanted you close enough to speak to me"; the card torn once under a saucer; "For thirty years." |
| The table | Lydia alone, briefly | Gathering nothing into a very small pile |

The novel's Henry is at the service bar. Only there does Ruth say "I've seen something
like it. At work," and only if Henry saw the knife touch the plate. It ends on the
episode's single spoken choice: **"Do you want me to ask him?"** — and if Ruth says no,
Henry may ask anyway. Paul, asked, does what the cast bible says he does: stops before
the end of a sentence and looks for Ruth. He gives Henry nothing. Ruth's "You have no
idea how much can be done with one syllable" becomes something shorter, and after one in
the morning she opens with "You asked him."

Paul's leaving is fixed: the key from beside his plate; the envelope rising from his
inside pocket and Ruth pushing it down with two fingers (a look); "Say it badly. Just
say it yourself."; the jacket, the handbag; "Watch my coffee." "It hasn't moved yet."
"Good." Henry watches the indicator reach three; Marian asks about husbands.

**The wait** is the novel's: Nell in Paul's chair, Lydia's half cup, Edwin's second pot,
Robert's two empty cups. Henry may look at the indicator when the lift begins to rise;
Nell does not.

**Ruth's return is not occluded.** It is the one moment the game lets the player see
everything, if they look, because it is the night's evidence. She steps out alone. In
the length of her walk to the bar, four things:

- her handbag under her arm instead of on it;
- a pale smear along the outside of one shoe — and Henry's reading of it, "dust from
  the carton," is the only reading the game offers; it does not correct him;
- the brass key from her jacket pocket to beside Edwin's hand;
- her coffee touched to her mouth and set down.

Then the exchange, as written: "He said not to wait." "He said the opposite to me." "He
changed his mind." "He wanted me to tell Nell for him. I refused." "He said he needed to
think." Two reports of what Paul said, in the same minute, that do not fit each other —
and after one in the morning a third, "he needed time." They are the first link of the
chain that ends in Chapter Fifteen. Henry can carry them or not.

### V. The window (Scenes 8–9) — the second room

The telephone; "Slowly, Mr. Bell."; "Is the person moving?"; Nell at the lift and Edwin
reaching it first; "I need someone who knows what not to touch." Ruth: "Henry." The lift
down through the dark departments.

The vestibule, then the court, then the stage. Inspect only; there is nothing to use.
Bell answers what he is asked and repeats it shorter.

| Where | Look | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| Court | the window from outside: the moon split and sagged, the man beneath, his shoe in the light, the six figures still facing the seventh place | the body; the window changed |
| Court | Bell without his cap | texture |
| Stage door | locked; Bell opened it far enough to see a face, locked it, called upstairs | the stage door |
| Stage | two fingers under the jaw | the touch (logged) |
| Stage | the gallery above: an open section of rail, a black rectangle; the shallow carton beside it | the carton, on the gallery |
| Stage | pale horizontal marks under the steel lip; one long scrape lower down | the marks; the scrape |
| Stage | a red button mounted beside the open rail | the button |
| Stage | a torn typed piece under the fingers of his right hand; the top edge reads INT. BEDROOM — NIGHT | the heading; not a letter |
| Vestibule | the plain door marked DISPLAY ACCESS, unlocked "while a window is unfinished" | the access door |
| Vestibule | through its wired glass: the enclosed stair turning up; something white tracked down the lower treads; the black opening at the top | the stair; the whiting |
| Bell | "He gave her the lift key"; "I went into receiving to make the closing call"; "I never opened the motor-court door" | the key's path; Bell's absence; no way out by the court |

The player may touch what the novel's Henry does not. Everything touched is logged, and
the first checkpoint is not Ward's: it is a uniformed officer asking "What did you
touch?" Henry answers from the log, truthfully, and Ward asks the same question again
five minutes later and writes the answer down. A Henry who pulled the paper free has a
longer answer and a shorter evening.

### VI. Names and addresses (Scenes 10–14) — the statement

**On the court**, Ward's "What did you see down here?" is answered from the board. The
novel's Henry gives the whole list — the locked stage door, the unlocked access door,
the whiting on the treads, the carton, the marks, the button, the heading — because the
novel's Henry looked at everything. The game's Henry gives the subset he has. Ward's
"Bell will tell me" and "I can see it" are the same either way.

**Upstairs**, the private dining room with the painted harbor: the episode's checkpoint.
Ward walks the night in order and at each beat Henry answers from what he carries. Three
kinds of answer are always available where the board allows: **what he saw**, **what he
thinks**, **nothing**. Ward hears the difference every time.

| Beat | Ward asks | Henry can give | The model line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ruth and the card | Did she know her brother was invited? | "Her reaction said no," as report (eighth card seen) or as guess | — |
| The door | Why did you stop the door? | "I don't know." Only that. | fixed |
| June's sentence | — (Ward does not ask; Henry may offer) | the sentence; the knife on the plate (if seen) | — |
| The envelope | — | Nell's name upside down; "Hear it"; Ruth's two fingers (if seen) | — |
| The key | Where did the key come from? | Edwin, before coffee, "after dessert" (if seen); or only that Paul had it | — |
| The descent | Who came down in the lift? | Paul and Ruth; the indicator at three | fixed |
| Her return | You said she touched it to her mouth. Did she drink? Was she frightened? | "She didn't drink the coffee." / "Yes." / "I don't think so." | "It is what I saw." |
| What Paul said | — | "not to wait" and "needed to think," both, one, or neither | — |
| The shoe | — | "There was dust on her shoe from the carton." Or nothing. | — |

Ward's close depends on the shape of the whole:

- mostly *what he saw*: "Keep doing that." and, at the door, "If you remember something,
  call me before you decide what it means." — the novel's lines, earned;
- mostly *what he thinks*: Ward closes the notebook earlier and says only "You are one
  of them."; the door line is not said;
- withheld heavily: the same as the novel's close, and the withheld set goes onto the
  board as **kept**, which is what the later episodes are about.

**At the passenger elevators**, Ruth's "What did you tell him?" has three answers:
the novel's "Ward told us not to compare statements"; "What I saw."; or "That you didn't
drink your coffee." The third breaks Ward's instruction and gives Ruth something — that
Henry watches her cup — and is carried as a fact for Thursday morning to answer. Her
account is the bible's, unchanged, whichever he says.

**In the motor court**, Nell asks what was in the window with him. Henry's answers are
observation or silence: "The carton was on the gallery. There was a torn typed page in
his hand." "The police have his clothing." The game offers no inference aloud here,
because Ruth is at the edge of the light and her Friday account depends on what she
does not yet know Henry has read. Nell's turn at the car — "You told me what you saw."
"And what you didn't." — is said only to a Henry who did exactly that. To any other
Henry she gets into the car without turning back.

Then Lydia's suitcase, Edwin with no keys, "I'll find a cab." "No. Not tonight." The
screen over the glass, the moon lit behind it. Henry entered Tolland's with Ruth. He
leaves alone. Fixed.

## What does not vary

The crime and its clock. Every line of the fixed sentences. Ruth's Wednesday account,
to Ward and to Henry. The order of the night. The release after one. The ending: Henry
leaves alone. There is no fork, and the episode should not feel as if it wanted one.

## What Henry carries into Episode Two

The board, exactly. Plus four carried acts: whether he asked Paul; what he told Ruth at
the elevators; what he gave Ward and what he kept; what he said to Nell. Episode Two is
Thursday (Chapters Seven to Nine): Nell on the landing at 9:20 with a retainer for
June's property, not for Paul's death; Ruth at ten with an address and a key; Lydia's
carton at 1:08; the Kenton Rooms at four; Alvarado at dusk, "Help me." "And I said no."
It opens on whatever Wednesday's board holds.

## New writing this episode needs

Everything not listed here is lifted from the novel verbatim.

- Paul's deflection when Henry asks about the sentence at the bar: four to six cues, in
  the pattern "stops before the end and waits for Ruth."
- Ruth's shorter line in place of "You have no idea how much can be done with one
  syllable," and her "You asked him." opening after one.
- The uniformed officer's "What did you touch?" exchange for a Henry who touched more:
  a handful of cues, and Ward's second asking.
- Ward's follow-ups per beat of the statement — an observation answer, an
  interpretation answer, and his response to each; the coffee exchange in the novel is
  the template for all of them. Roughly ten beats, two or three cues each.
- Ward's two alternative closes.
- Ruth's response to "What I saw." and to "That you didn't drink your coffee." at the
  elevators: two short exchanges.
- Nell's car without the turn: action only, no new cue.
- Henry's own answer lines at each choice, in his voice: short, never "I think."

Order of size: 150 to 250 new cues against roughly 800 lifted. The strands at the table
and at coffee need no new dialogue at all; the novel already wrote every place Henry
might be.

## Decisions made without asking

- The episode ends at the cab, not at the window. The statements are the episode's
  reason; the body alone is a cutscene.
- No fork. The endings are for the accusation and belong to a later episode.
- The shoe is a misread, not a clue. The game records what Henry saw, in Henry's words,
  and lets Friday correct it.
- Ruth's return is not occluded, on purpose, and the table courses are.
- Henry may not infer aloud in Ruth's hearing. The bible's account sequence is worth more
  than one extra dialogue option.
- Attention is per course, not per line. A course is the unit at which the room is
  legibly doing more than one thing.

## Held for comparison

- Occluding Ruth's return as well (two of four looks). Rejected for now: it punishes the
  one moment the game most needs the player to have seen.
- A choice at the office: Henry declines the supper and Ruth goes alone. Rejected: the
  Henry rule; and the office is the one scene the game should let be a play.
- Henry at the service bar with Edwin and Robert during the descent, instead of watching
  the indicator. Kept as a fifth place in the coffee movement; it costs the indicator at
  three and buys Edwin on orange ice.
