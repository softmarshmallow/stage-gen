# The Grain — cast bible

Status: **derived from the finished draft, 2026-09-02**. Supersedes
`cast/opening-quartet-v0.md` and `cast/remaining-four-v1-relational-web.md` wherever
they differ; those are kept as history. Ages are for September 1972.

## Henry Calder — private investigator, "Calder Investigations"

Late forties *(inferred; a contemporary of Ward's)*. One room over a street with a
traffic signal; two client chairs that do not match; a filing cabinet that lists; a
fan; a card in the frosted glass he turns at 5:37. Finds husbands, "where they said
they weren't." Met Ruth on the old case six years ago and has seen her "several
times since"; has never given her his home address because she never asked.

**Wants.** At first, nothing: to sit at a dinner and not be employed. Then, to find
what Paul brought back. Underneath, to hear Ruth say it rather than find it, and
to be allowed to keep doing that.
**Arc.** From the hand in the door ("I don't know" why) to "This time I found it."
He stops listening to what she says about a fact and reads the fact. The cost is
the one refutation he cannot say to her face (the shoe), and a train he misses.
**Voice.** Asks. Short questions, rarely a statement longer than a line. Dry
without wit for its own sake ("It's the same coffee as this morning"). Does not
explain his own feelings; the narration does it for him, sparingly. Never says
"I think."
**Objects.** The two cups; the legal pad with three questions; the pocketknife;
the card in the door; Lydia's green-ink place card, kept where the knife is.

## Ruth Ellery — copywriter, "Ruth Ellery, Copywriting," West Third

About forty *(inferred: Paul is "younger by only a few years")*. Her name on the
door is larger than Henry's; she ordered the door. Writes product copy, funeral-home
pamphlets ("At a time like this"), and, for four months, the best dialogue of her
life for a picture she was not told the name of. Paul's older sister; the one he
always asked. Lives in a pale stucco block with a dry fountain.

**Wants.** To be heard before she is found out. To be the one who says the
sentence. Not to have her name on the page in Paul's hand.
**Arc.** Five accounts. She starts by volunteering the truth "in whole sentences"
so Henry will not decide what she kept; ends by keeping one sentence against her
lawyer's advice "so it would be the shape it is." She does not want forgiveness
and says so through a fence.
**Voice.** Epigram as reflex ("I remembered you were a liar"; "I removed nothing.
I brought something in"), and the book's rule is that she must sometimes hear
herself do it and stop ("I'm doing it again"; "That's the copy"). Reads upside
down. Says "Good" and means it. Under pressure her sentences get shorter, not
longer; when a sentence breaks it is because the second half is true.
**Tells.** Sets a coffee cup on the floor; touches a cup to her mouth without
drinking; pushes an envelope down in a pocket with two fingers; puts a coat on
one sleeve then the other without help.
**The crime.** She followed Paul for her fifth cover, refused to write him one
more sentence, took hold of the loose set, tore it, pushed him with both hands,
held his wrist, saw the button, chose the pages, said no twice, let go. "It was
that small."

## Nell Avery — June's daughter, Paul's former wife

Twenty-nine (birthday in August). Cut her hair in June. Eats when she is hungry
and says so. Lives at "the old address," the apartment where the hall closet held
JUNE — WORK. Works at something that requires gloves she rubs at (never
specified; keep it unspecified).

**Wants.** To be told badly, once, by someone who will not make it bearable. To
decide about her mother's voice herself, since nobody asked her at fifteen or at
twenty-nine.
**Arc.** Hires Henry to find property and finds the whole of it: her mother
working, her husband's theft, Ruth's sentence, and then Ruth. Refuses Havel's
completion ("Nobody owns a room"), refuses Lydia's, refuses Ruth's forgiveness
question by never asking it. Her one lie is retracted before anyone looks.
**Voice.** Flat. Declarative. Corrects the tense nobody helps her with. "Those
things can both be true" (borrowed from Lydia, returned sharper). Does not
raise her voice; her long speeches are lists (what Paul took; what she would
recognize). "Good," learned from Ruth in the corridor and kept.
**Objects.** The gloves; the good frying pan; the green school notebook and
Ward's pencil, returned to the bench.

## Lydia Shaw — alterations, Tolland's, twenty-six years

Sixties *(inferred; a contemporary of Marian, "middle fifties," or a little older)*.
Green ink; pin marks on her fingers; a dress in which nothing is accidental. Kept
continuity and wardrobe on *Private Weather* and kept its archive in her workroom
for fourteen years. Leaving for a rented house in Portland with a dark blue door.

**Wants.** To finish the story before she leaves: return June's material to Nell
through Paul, forgive Ruth in front of everyone "so that it would count," be
loved by all of them in compatible ways for one evening.
**Arc.** Her method fails at the first course and kills a man by the coffee; she
misses the train, identifies the reels, and cannot forgive at the new size. Leaves
on the Monday train without being seen off, having told Edwin two versions of the
same sentence and chosen neither.
**Voice.** Composed, then work. Begins speeches with a shape ("It began with June,
and it went through all of you in order") and is cut off, by Nell or by herself,
before the end. Never reaches the end of the kitchen-table story. "Would you have
come?" three times and it is counted against her.
**Objects.** Place cards, numbers crossed out twice; the cinnamon tin; the
unopened suitcase; the transfer tag she asked for.

## Marian Holt — June's closest friend

Middle fifties. Handsome; dressed for a better restaurant than the one she
remembers. Held June's coat every day on the set. Kept the photographs; kept
JUNE — WORK in her garage eleven years; asked twice that the recordings be burned
so nobody would find June's voice after she could no longer say no.

**Wants.** June left with June. Robert to say no once. Nell at her house.
**Arc.** Learns Robert lied to her for Nell's sake and, in Paul's declaration,
that he threatened to tell Nell himself; finds nothing to correct and says "Yes,
Robert" in the other key. Hears June cough on a tape and says "Go on."
**Voice.** Corrects: numbers (eleven, not nine; thirty, not twenty-eight), places
(Burbank), quantities (cinnamon), tenses. Short. The correction is love and
control at once. When she has nothing to correct she is silent, and that is the
turn.
**Tells.** Salt passed unused; a hand on Robert's sleeve; gloves in both hands.

## Robert Holt — Marian's husband

Grayer than his wife, broad. Has known Lydia thirty years (he says twenty-eight).
Answers the telephone. Drives and waits with the engine off. Apologizes for the
traffic since December 1958.

**Wants.** To be on time; to bring the water before it is asked for; for Nell to
decide.
**Arc.** The book's quiet brave man: took Paul's number, took Paul a sandwich,
moved the archive, and told Paul he would tell Nell himself. Says nothing about the
chicken on Sunday, and is loved for it.
**Voice.** Rambles; starts a story from the wrong end and is corrected; keeps
going ("I don't know why I'm telling you that"). "That sounds right." Never
finishes the sentence about why Marian wanted the number changed.

## Paul Ellery — Ruth's brother, Nell's former husband

About thirty-six, younger than Ruth "by only a few years." Knew the Avery–Holt circle from his early twenties (he was at June's table for the burned chicken, 1958, and has had the Holts' number "since Pasadena"); married Nell about 1969. Looks younger than that in the
instant before he recognizes a room. Takes things apart carefully and leaves the
pieces in the order they go back. Cannot put a fan together. Eats capers at
Ruth's, not at Nell's; "I had somewhere to put them." Stops before the end of a
sentence and waits for Ruth.

**Wanted.** To say it himself, badly, once, and have it be his. Typed the Nell
paragraph so that if he stopped halfway there would be a record that he knew the
condition.
**Function.** The book's victim and its only person who finishes correcting
himself. His declaration is the reading at the second table. His last two words
are his sister's name and "help me."

## Edwin Price — the Winter Room

Narrow, silver-haired, a knee that "remains mine." Believes seven is an accusation
and eight a dinner party; there is no room for eight, he measured. Has written
four things for Lydia in twenty-six years, none of which left the building until
the fourth.

**Voice.** Formal to the point of comedy, and precise ("Thank you for making both
remarks"). Two callbacks are his and no more.
**Function.** Keys, doors, bolts, the closed building; the man who gave Paul the
lift; the man who makes Bell sit; the messenger for the place card.

## Mr. Bell — night guard

About sixty. Forgets his cap and then carries it. Logs doors. Four minutes on the
closing call, fourteen on the round. Opened the stage door only far enough to see
a face. Never opened the motor-court door for Paul; opens it for Ruth twice, and
for Henry once when it is not even closed.

**Voice.** Repeats. Says a thing correctly and then says it again, shorter ("Four
minutes. Call it four. The night desk reads back every door, so it's four").
**Function.** The honest inventory; the timing; "There wasn't a bell."

## Detective Ellis Ward

Early fifties, brown suit that has outlasted fashion. Knows Henry from before.
Wants the rail. Does not need a notebook after Wednesday. Lets Henry be "her one
person" and lets Ruth say it to Henry in his hearing "because I want to know
whether it changes."

**Voice.** Blunt, procedural, one joke per chapter, one line of feeling per book
("Then that's the one"). Never explains a numbered card.

## Miriam Havel — associate producer, *A Single Light*

Forty-four; twenty-two years in the business. Wrote VERIFICATION PENDING and let
it become yes. Called Lydia and did not stay for the answer. Loses the order in
which she meant to speak exactly once, when she meets R.E. Wanted two things in
her career the way she wants that room.

## Mrs. Vale — landlady, the Kenton Rooms

Small, gray house dress, keys at her waist. Writes both names on the inventory.
Liked Paul enough to be angry. Comes to the funeral.

## Offstage

- **June Avery** (1917–December 1958). Forty-one at her death; heart. Backward
  capital J, corrected. Wrote her name on things so Marian would stop putting them
  away. Two sentences survive her: "Don't improve it just because it's over" and
  "Coming indoors isn't coming back. I only said he could come in."
- **Arthur Rusk.** Directed *Private Weather* for nine days in 1958 and *A Single
  Light* in 1972. Kept one workprint. Cut the man's last question. Never appears
  and never meets Ruth.
- **Frances.** From the old case; sends Ruth a Christmas card to the old address.
  Never explained; texture, kept deliberately.
