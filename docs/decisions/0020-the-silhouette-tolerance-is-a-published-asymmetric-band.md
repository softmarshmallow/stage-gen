# 0020 — The drawn silhouette may leave the authored one by a published, asymmetric band

*Ruled 2026-09-03 with the painted-ground spike.*

## Fact

The runner's ground validator proves every solid cell opaque to its last pixel and every empty
cell transparent to its first. On a hunting map's occupancy that rule publishes one
razor-straight line across fifty-six tiles and eleven perfect rectangles — *more* rectilinear
than the tile atlas already shipping, whose cells carry transparent contours and lifted caps.

## Challenge

Exact alpha is the strongest possible guarantee, and any tolerance is a hole in it.

## Ruling

The drawn edge may leave the authored one by a bounded, published, asymmetric band: eight
pixels inward everywhere, sixteen outward at a side or an underside, eight outward over a
walking surface. Drawn wider than collision reads as moss over the feet; drawn narrower puts a
body on visible air; and an overhang above a surface is the one outward error a player reads a
jump against. It is a *presentation* bound, and it travels in the manifest as a silhouette
tolerance so a debug overlay can say how far the art may stray. Leakage is measured per cell
and again along the two structures that matter — the gaps a player jumps through, and the air
a deck hangs in — because a hunting map is 71 per cent air and a model can fill every hop gap
completely while still scoring 0.07 on a whole-grid mean.

## Evidence

Two things the spike taught that reading could not. The band had to be measured against the
cell's *core* rather than the whole cell, because a deck's end cell is exposed on two sides at
once and a correctly organic silhouette rounds that corner, which a whole-cell floor reads as
a missing floor. And the guide's row window needs a bottom bleed: a hard bottom edge reads as
a mass that ends there, and the first two paintings duly gave the bank a handsome rocky
underside and pulled it up off the row that meets the bottom of the screen.

## Falsifier

A tolerance a player can read as a lie — a jump attempted against drawn ground that is not
there, or a body standing on painted air — inside the published band.
