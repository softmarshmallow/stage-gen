# 0025 — CookieRun is adopted for its level language and refused for its architecture

*Ruled at the opening of the runner adoption.*

## Fact

The runner draws uniformly from every eligible chunk, so the chunk that teaches and the chunk
that tests cannot be ordered and every telegraph must work on first sight. Lookahead is
fifteen columns — 2.50s at base speed, 1.67s fully ramped — against a terminal consequence.

## Challenge

Canabalt is the closer structural mirror: procedural, un-memorisable, one verb. Its famous
rule, that the maximum gap is a live function of current speed, is the obvious thing to adopt.

## Ruling

Canabalt's rule is the runtime-heuristic version of a proof this repository already runs
offline and earlier, so picking it would mostly teach us we are already right. CookieRun is
adopted for the thing genuinely missing: a level language whose vocabulary matches the verb
set, written as a collectible trail that is *routing rather than decoration* — greed and
survival point the same way, so the player is never asked to learn the level. What is refused
is its architecture: hand-built episodes whose fairness guarantee is a human seeing the next
forty columns, and per-stage economy tuning, because every one of those dials is an authored
number and this contract has none.

## Evidence

The trail device arrives with nothing bolted to it, unlike the alternatives surveyed —
contextual verbs need non-local state the seam rule forbids, and the rhythm references arrive
welded to a beat contract. The pickup shape was already `{item_id, column, row}`, capped per
chunk, streamed and scored: the whole mechanic is expressible at zero contract cost and zero
art cost, and the committed fixture was spending its pickups on three decorative dots.

## Falsifier

A fairness property the offline admission proof cannot express but a human reading forty
columns can — which would mean the refused architecture buys something the adopted one does
not.
