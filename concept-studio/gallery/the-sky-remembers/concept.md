# The Sky Remembers

> Status: selected pre-production game concept only; not a game package, runtime contract, or
> implementation plan.

## Pitch

An 18-year-old student crosses her rural hometown after school beneath an impossibly blue summer
sky, following a bell melody only she remembers and carrying proof of a younger brother whom
everyone else has forgotten.

## World

The story takes place in a present-day regional Japanese-inspired town shaped by rice fields, a
single-track electric railway, a public high school, concrete drainage channels, neighborhood
shops, paved lanes, guardrails, bicycles, power poles, and low modern homes. Students call the
local railway the **Horizon Line** because it seems to run straight into the enormous blue above
the fields.

The town is neither medieval nor ruined. Its supernatural quality appears when familiar places
become subtly incomplete. When the last person forgets a face, home, or promise, its shape appears
for a few seconds among the summer clouds and then dissolves into the blue. Shared sounds seem to
hold memories in place: school bells, railway crossings, bicycle bells, station announcements, and
the municipal chime that plays every afternoon at five.

Those sounds have begun dropping out. A crossing flashes without ringing. A station sign becomes
blank. A desk disappears from a classroom, and by the next morning nobody remembers who sat there.
The world remains radiant, ordinary, windy, and painfully beautiful; the clearer the day, the more
exposed every absence feels.

## Protagonist

The protagonist is an 18-year-old student in her final year of secondary school. One year ago, she
promised to collect her younger brother after school, but stayed late and told him to walk home
alone. At the railway crossing she found only his faded red scarf caught on the barrier. By the
following morning, her family, teachers, photographs, school records, and the town itself had
rearranged around the belief that he had never existed.

Only she remembers him. She keeps his scarf tied to her school satchel and carries a small brass
bell charm he once gave her. Now the five-o'clock municipal chime has begun playing the private
melody he used to tap against her bedroom wall, coming each afternoon from a speaker farther east
along the Horizon Line.

She begins walking. Her emotional question is larger than whether she can find him: **is
remembering someone an act of love, or can it become a way of refusing to live?**

## Player fantasy and journey

The player is a small, determined student crossing a familiar luminous town after school. The
journey moves through roads, field paths, platforms, crossings, canal edges, and school corridors
along the Horizon Line, recovering half-erased stories and learning why the town has begun to
forget. Its essential rhythm is forward motion, discovery, brief human connection, and quiet
release. The concept allows moments of danger and physical traversal, but does not yet assume
combat or define runtime behavior.

## Visual identity

- Working style label: **luminous pastoral pixel painting**—modern high-color atmospheric pixel
  art with painterly environmental composition.
- Side-view staging with a low horizon and an immense cobalt-to-cerulean sky occupying most of the
  frame.
- Dense, coherent pixel clusters; banded shading; crisp silhouettes; minimal dark contour; sparse
  dithering; and no smooth airbrushed modeling.
- Monumental sunlit cumulus clouds, lavender and cool-blue cloud shadows, golden fields, and small
  faded-red accents.
- Contemporary railways, crossing signals, bicycles, guardrails, concrete channels, power lines,
  school buildings, and low homes make the era unmistakable without crowding the sky.
- Long horizontal field edges, rails, roads, and wires pull the eye toward the next place.
- The mood is expansive, tranquil, nostalgic, and faintly bittersweet. Its beauty should feel
  sincere rather than cute.

## Selected cover

The user selected Candidate 04, **Silent Level Crossing**, from the reviewed cover batch. It shows
one proportional, non-sexualized student in profile at a modern rural crossing, with her brother's
red scarf and bell charm, a distant school, and a half-dissolved rooftop hidden in the monumental
cloud bank. The horizontal rails and low horizon preserve the intended side-scrolling read while
the blue sky carries the emotional weight.

`images/cover.webp` is the independently reviewed, storage-compliant gallery preview. The full
selected PNG remains in the ignored concept workspace as the human-facing working handoff. This
cover is visual concept evidence, not proof that the depicted game has been implemented.

## Open choice

The most consequential unresolved choice is what the moving five-o'clock melody means: the
brother may truly be calling from somewhere beyond the last station, or the journey may lead the
student toward accepting that he is gone. That choice determines whether the game ultimately
becomes a mystery of reunion or a pilgrimage through grief.
